The Student's Guide to Validating Ideas Cheaply

You don't need money to test if people want your product. Here are 5 ways to validate for free.

Most student startups fail not because they couldn't build the product, but because they built something nobody wanted. Validation is the art of verifying your assumptions before writing a single line of code.

1. The Mom Test

Don't ask your mom if your idea is good—she loves you and will lie to you. Instead, ask potential customers about their past behaviors and problems. If they haven't tried to solve the problem themselves, it might not be painful enough to pay for.

2. Landing Page Mockups

Use tools like Carrd or Wix to build a simple one-page site describing your product. Add a "Join Waitlist" button. If nobody signs up, you just saved yourself months of development time.

3. Community Polls

Post your problem statement in relevant Reddit communities, Discord servers, or college WhatsApp groups. Don't pitch the solution yet. See if the problem resonates.

4. The "Fake Door" Test

Create a button on your site that says "Buy Now" or "Download". When clicked, show a message saying "We are launching soon! Enter email to get notified." This measures high-intent interest.
